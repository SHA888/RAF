## Part 1: Preprint Strategy (IMMEDIATE - DO THIS FIRST)

### Why arXiv Now?

✅ **Advantages**:
- **Instant publication** (24-48h review turnaround)
- **Timestamps our work** (priority for future citations)
- **Community feedback** before formal submission
- **Builds credibility** ("preprint on arXiv")

## Part 6: Manuscript Template (Adapt from README)

### Abstract (250 words max)

```
Reciprocal acceleration between Quantum Computing (QC) and Machine Learning (ML)
is a key driver of near-term quantum advantage. However, systematic understanding
of QC-ML co-evolution remains limited. Here we introduce the Reciprocal Acceleration
Framework (RAF), a methodology for formalizing and analyzing feedback loops between
QC and ML at three computational levels: error mitigation (output level), ansatz
design (circuit level), and calibration-control (hardware level).

Using empirical studies on simulated NISQ devices (5–27 qubits with device-calibrated
noise), we demonstrate how improvements in one loop accelerate progress in others
through cross-loop coupling. We identify rate-limiting bottlenecks and show that
investments in neural surrogate models and standardized benchmarks provide
high-leverage impact across all three loops.

Our key finding: optimizations in calibration-control yield 0.7–0.8 strength coupling
to both error mitigation and ansatz design, making hardware-level improvements
a high-leverage investment. Cross-loop coupling validates the feedback loop hypothesis.

RAF is open-source and supports multiple quantum backends (Qiskit, AWS Braket,
Azure Quantum, IQM). This work provides systematic guidance for quantum-AI research
prioritization and a foundation for real-world hardware validation.
```
